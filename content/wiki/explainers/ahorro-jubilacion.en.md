---
title: "How the retirement savings calculator works"
---

This calculator projects the capital you'll have at retirement by combining what you've already saved, what you contribute each month and the expected return until then. Unlike the FIRE calculator (which reasons in real terms), here you use a **nominal** return (effective annual, before inflation) and inflation is modelled separately, so you also see your wealth in today's euros.

**How to read the result.** The *nominal wealth* is the figure you'll see in your account on the day you retire. The *wealth in today's euros* discounts inflation: it's what that money will actually buy. The *monthly income* applies the 4% rule to the real wealth, so it's already in today's purchasing power.

**Worked example** (default values). At 30, with €15,000 saved and €300/month until 67 (37 years), with a 6 % nominal return, 2.5 % inflation and 0.3 % fees (TER):

- Contributed: €15,000 + (€300 × 12 × 37) = **€148,200**. The return is an effective annual one: with the same percentage, changing the contribution frequency only changes when the money goes in, not what it earns.
- Estimated nominal wealth: about **€555,693**, thanks to compound interest.
- In today's euros that's about **€222,870** (inflation takes the rest).
- It would fund an income of about **€742.90/month** in today's purchasing power (the 4 % rule).

**An important note.** The public pension likely won't be enough to keep your standard of living; private saving complements it. Future return, inflation and fees are assumptions, not a guarantee: adjust them to explore different scenarios.
