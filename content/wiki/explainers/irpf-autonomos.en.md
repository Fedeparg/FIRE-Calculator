---
title: "How the self-employed income-tax calculator works"
---

A self-employed person is taxed not on what they bill but on their **net income**: revenue minus deductible expenses minus their Social Security contribution. The income-tax scale applies to that net, less your **personal and family minimum** (age, children, ascendants, disability and joint taxation), which lowers the tax.

**How to read the result.** The *net income* is your real profit. The *estimated income tax* is what you'd pay. The *effective rate* is the average; the *marginal rate* is what you'd pay on the next euro.

**Worked example** (default values). You bill €40,000, with €8,000 of expenses and €4,000 of self-employed contributions, single, age 30, no children:

- Net income: 40,000 − 8,000 − 4,000 = **€28,000**.
- Personal and family minimum: **€5,550**.
- Estimated income tax: **€5,511** (effective rate **19.7 %**, marginal rate **30 %**).

The self-employed contribution is deductible: the more you contribute, the lower your base. And adding dependent children or ascendants lowers the tax.

**An important note.** It excludes quarterly instalments and activity-specific reductions (e.g. the hard-to-justify expenses of simplified direct assessment). It's indicative and not tax advice.
