---
title: "How the fixed-term deposit calculator works"
---

A **fixed-term deposit** locks up your money for a period in exchange for known interest (the APR). It's among the safest products, but also among the least profitable.

**How to read the result.** You'll see the *gross interest*, the *net final value* and, if you enter an inflation figure, the *real value* (what that money is worth today). The interest is taxed in the savings base (19 % withholding, adjustable).

**Worked example** (default values). €10,000 at a 3 % APR for 1 year, with 2.5 % inflation:

- Gross interest: **€300**.
- After the 19 % withholding: **€243** net (final value **€10,243**).
- Real value (today's purchasing power): 10,243 / 1.025 ≈ **€9,993**.

**An important note.** Here's the catch: you earn €243 net, but with 2.5 % inflation your money **loses purchasing power** (from €10,000 real to €9,993). Always compare the interest with expected inflation.
