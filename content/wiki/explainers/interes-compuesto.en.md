---
title: "How the compound interest calculator works"
---

This calculator projects how much your investment could be worth by combining three forces: the **initial capital**, the **regular contributions** and the **annual return** that gets reinvested. There are two different frequencies: the **contribution frequency** (how often you add money) and the **interest compounding** (how often interest is credited and starts earning interest itself).

**How to read the result.** The *final value* splits into what you put in (*total contributed*) and what the money generated on its own (*interest earned*). Over the long run, that second part usually ends up the larger.

**Worked example** (default values). You start with €5,000, add €300 a month at 7 % annually for 25 years, with annual compounding (the default option):

- Total contributed: €5,000 + (€300 × 12 × 25) = **€95,000**.
- Estimated final value: about **€262,050**.
- Interest earned: about **€167,050**, nearly double what you contributed.

**Contribution frequency vs. compounding.** The contribution frequency only decides *when* your money goes in (at the end of each period): with a contribution of 0, changing it does not alter the result. Compounding does: with *annual*, the percentage is the effective annual return (€10,000 at 7 % is €10,700 after a year); with *monthly*, the percentage is a nominal rate split by 12 (like a TIN), so €10,000 at 7 % becomes €10,722.90. With the example above and monthly compounding the final value would be about €271,649. If your product quotes a TAE (APR/APY), use annual compounding; if it quotes a TIN credited monthly, use monthly.

**An important note.** The return is an **estimate**, not a guarantee. To think in real purchasing power, subtract inflation (7 % nominal − 3 % ≈ 4 % real).
