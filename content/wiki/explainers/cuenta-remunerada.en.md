---
title: "How the high-yield account calculator works"
---

A **high-yield account** pays interest on the money you hold in it while keeping immediate access (unlike a deposit, which locks it up).

**How to read the result.** You'll see the *interest* at the stated APR, the *net final balance* and, if you enter an inflation figure, the *real value* of that balance. As with deposits, the interest is taxed in the savings base (19 % withholding, adjustable).

**Worked example** (default values). €10,000 at a 2.5 % APR for 1 year, with 2.5 % inflation:

- Gross interest: **€250**.
- After the 19 % withholding: **€202.50** net (final balance **€10,202.50**).
- Real value (today's purchasing power): 10,202.50 / 1.025 ≈ **€9,954**.

Here the withholding and inflation eat the whole return: the balance grows, but your purchasing power falls from €10,000 to about €9,954.

**An important note.** Read the small print: many accounts cap the rewarded balance, require a salary direct debit, or pay the high rate for only a few months. And compare the interest with inflation.
