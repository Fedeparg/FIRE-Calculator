# Capital gains when selling: how they are calculated and filed

When you sell fund units, shares or an ETF, what is taxed is not what you receive but
the **capital gain or loss**: the difference between what you get and what the units
you sold cost you. Simple enough, until you have bought on several dates at several
prices. That is where the rules on this page come in.

## Transfer value minus acquisition value

- **Acquisition value**: what you paid for the units you sold **plus** the purchase
  fees and costs.
- **Transfer value**: what you receive for the sale **minus** the sale fees and
  costs.
- **Gain (or loss)** = transfer − acquisition.

Fees count on both sides: they raise the cost and lower the proceeds, so they reduce
the gain. If a purchase of 100 units had a €10 fee and you sell 40, €4 of it belongs
to those 40.

## FIFO: the oldest units always go first

For **homogeneous** securities (shares of the same company, units of the same fund or
ETF) Spanish law requires **FIFO** (*first in, first out*): you are deemed to sell the
units you bought **first**. You cannot pick the most expensive lot to pay less, nor
use the average price.

An often-missed detail: FIFO applies to **all your units of that security, whichever
broker holds them**. If you bought an ETF with one broker in 2020 and the same ETF
with another in 2023, and you sell with the second, the 2020 units go first for tax
purposes. How many units each broker holds does not change; the cost used to work
out the gain does.

## Offsetting: losses subtract

The gains and losses of **all your sales in the same year** are added together. If
in one year you gain €3,000 selling a fund and lose €1,000 selling some shares, you
are taxed on €2,000.

If the year ends **negative**:

1. That negative balance can offset up to **25%** of the positive balance of
   interest and dividends in the same year.
2. Whatever remains is **carried forward for four years** to offset future gains.

A positive balance is taxed in the [savings base](/aprende/fiscalidad-del-ahorro) in
bands: 19% up to €6,000, 21% up to €50,000, 23% up to €200,000, 27% up to €300,000
and 30% above that.

## The two-month rule

If you sell at a **loss** and buy units of the **same security** within the two
months before or after (listed securities), that loss **cannot be offset yet**: it is
recognised when you sell the repurchased units. The rule targets selling at a loss
just to save tax and buying back the next day.

## Sales in another currency

If the security trades in dollars (or another currency), the Spanish tax authority
asks for two separate calculations (DGT binding ruling V0152-26):

- **The gain on the shares** is calculated **in dollars** (sale minus purchase) and
  that difference is converted to euros at the exchange rate of the **sale date**.
- **The exchange difference** on the dollars invested is a separate gain or loss: what
  they cost you in euros when you bought versus what they are worth in euros when you
  sell.

If your broker converts to euros when you buy and when you sell, both together add up
to converting the purchase at its date's rate and the sale at its own. That is why you
can gain in dollars and lose in euros (or vice versa) if the dollar has moved. If you
keep the dollars in an account, the exchange difference is not declared until you
convert them.

## Investment funds: switching

Between Spanish-registered **investment funds** you can move money from one to
another with a **switch** (*traspaso*) without paying tax: the gain is deferred until
you really sell. ETFs generally cannot be switched: selling one to buy another is a
sale.

## Sextante's capital gains report

If you record your purchases and sales in the [portfolio](/portfolio), the **Capital
gains** section (in the portfolio header) prepares the year's summary for you:

- It matches each sale against its purchases with **FIFO per security**, across all
  your brokers, with purchase fees spread across the units.
- It **nets** gains and losses within the tax year and estimates the tax with the
  savings scale.
- It converts sales in other currencies to euros at the **ECB reference rate** of the
  sale date and works out the exchange difference separately, as explained above.
- It lets you **download a CSV** with one row per sale (date, transfer value,
  acquisition value and gain, in the currency and in euros), which is the detail the
  tax return asks for.

What it does **not** do, and you should check yourself: carrying forward losses from
previous years, the 25% offset against dividends and interest and the two-month rule.
